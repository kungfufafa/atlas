# Independent review of prospective study options

**Recommendation:** the fixed-family, equal-quota KL loss bound is a defensible conservative option for a new, narrowly defined study. It is not ready for adoption until the task frame, independent unit, endpoint, sample size/power target, practical margin and candidate-selection rule are fixed. Keep all current studies and scores unchanged. I reviewed the proposal, calculator source and recorded numeric output; I did not execute the calculator, tests, simulations, native workers or paid inference, or inspect current scored/holdout outcomes. I have inherited earlier development context and do not claim global blinding.

## Analytic and numeric assessment

The loss-only algebra is correct: with paired losses q=P(A=0,H=1), wins r=P(A=1,H=0), the success difference is r−q≥−q. A valid upper bound Uq<.05 therefore suffices for strict 5pp NI; it cannot prove superiority and may fail even when the actual difference is zero.

For equal fixed family quotas and independent Bernoulli pair losses, the proposal's MGF/Jensen derivation correctly replaces heterogeneous probabilities by their arithmetic mean. Equal quotas make that mean the equal-family estimand. Inverting the KL lower-tail inequality produces a conservative upper bound without assuming the pooled count is binomial. Independence across pairs and a fixed sampling/stopping design are essential; independence of the two products inside a pair is not. This agrees with the independent bounded-variable framework in [Hoeffding (1963)](https://www.cs.rpi.edu/academics/courses/spring06/random/hoefding.pdf). The PDF was accessible; my assessment of this particular KL formula rests on the proposal's explicit derivation, not a claimed new full-text theorem audit.

The iid-mixture option correctly uses exact binomial inversion; it must not be substituted for heterogeneous fixed quotas. [NIST's exact-binomial documentation](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman2/auxillar/exacbino.htm) supports the one-sided inversion distinction. The calculator uses strict inequalities consistently, excludes k/n≥.05, and uses the binomial power sum only under its stated homogeneous scenario. The displayed grids agree with the retained JSON; the zero-loss thresholds agree with the closed-form reasoning. I found no arithmetic/definition discrepancy by source inspection. This is **not** an independent numerical validation of every entry or a production-ready statistical implementation. A future implementation still needs numerical/boundary tests; the current recurrence assumes interior probabilities and a modest fixed grid, and should not silently become a general arbitrary-n API.

## Certified completion can create misleading factual parity

Defining Y=1 as correct delivered completion **with** the required resource certificate is mathematically legitimate if declared in advance. An observed lack of the certificate can then be Y=0. It answers a different question from factual correctness. The distinction resembles defining an event as part of a composite endpoint rather than treating the underlying outcome as missing; [ICH E9(R1), section A.3.2](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/E9-R1_Step4_Guideline_2019_1203.pdf) discusses that general estimand distinction, not software benchmarking.

A prospective hypothetical shows that the existing quality floors do not prevent confusion: on 90% of tasks both products are correct and certified; on 10%, Atlas is wrong while Hermes is correct but uncertified. Certified-completion rates are 90% for both and q_cert=0, even though factual accuracy is 90% versus 100%. This pattern can hold in every family. Loss-only certified NI can therefore qualify with the observed 90%/80% floors while factual NI is false by 10pp. This is an illustration, not a statement about observed products.

For the user's goal, report the full paired factual outcome table alongside the certified-completion table and separate format, delivery, lifecycle, usage and evaluator-failure reasons. Do not score only the successful/certified subset. If the headline includes factual parity, require a prospectively valid factual NI guard too; certification NI alone is insufficient. If a factual outcome is unknown, a conservative loss sensitivity assigns a loss whenever A=0,H=1 remains feasible, rather than treating the pair as a tie. Wrong model/source/identity or a known asymmetric evaluator defect is a design-validity problem, not something a missingness convention repairs.

The certified endpoint may support a limited claim about that exact product–model–transport–evaluator setup. It cannot establish complete cost totals, actual consumption bounds on uncertified attempts, or a harness-only causal advantage. Missing usage remains unknown, and all attempts remain in the ledger. Do not remove current mandatory-accounting gates retroactively; any new endpoint/failure policy requires a new protocol before new results.

## Recommended next study, prospectively

1. **One candidate and one specified execution profile.** Select the candidate using development evidence, then freeze it before drawing the new confirmation sample. Use the same endpoint/model/header/capabilities/budget and audited observation rules for both arms. A later candidate must not reuse inspected confirmation as untouched evidence or repeatedly spend an unadjusted .05 until one qualifies.
2. **Operator-relevant fixed task frame.** Independently author and audit the complete frame before sampling. Equal weighting means category-macro performance, not typical daily-work frequency. Obtain operator judgment of task relevance and the 5pp tolerance; merely generating 100 name/number variants is insufficient. Preserve exclusion criteria and every draw. No substitution of a hard sampled case, removal of an outage, or adaptive extension until success.
3. **Equal quotas with independently sampled cases and one paired trajectory per draw.** Sampling with replacement within each fixed family gives the proposed target and analytic bound. An accidental repeated draw is part of that declared sampling design; deliberately repeating a chosen case to inflate n is not. Predeclare execution order across families and adjacent randomized arm order. Fresh state alone does not prove independence: shared provider/time shocks and persistent caches need an explicit treatment. If independent case blocks cannot be defended, analyze at justified independent blocks or keep the study descriptive; do not count arbitrary correlated attempts as independent.
4. **Choose the endpoint before n.** Prefer correct delivered completion as the task-ability endpoint, with certification reported distinctly. If certified completion is chosen as the operational primary endpoint, name it explicitly and require the separate factual guard for any broader “parity” headline. Keep memory-default and explicit-memory conditions separate. Cold/identity/process checks do not inflate the primary sample and must retain their own diagnostic outcomes.
5. **Plan fixed n from plausible discordance and power.** The zero-loss 72/88 thresholds are best-case passing counts, not execution recommendations. The proposal itself gives only about 48.5%/41.3% NI-only power at q=.01 for those counts. Its larger homogeneous grids still omit heterogeneous loss vectors, absolute-floor qualification and common shocks. Set an acceptable power target and plausible range first; if resources cannot support it, run a clearly descriptive study rather than promise proof.
6. **Lock the claim family.** The proposed .025/.0125/.0125 one-sided allocation is valid by a union bound for three predeclared valid directional decisions. It does not cover selecting among additional models, candidates, endpoints or new studies. For multiple future candidate confirmations, use a declared error-spending/family plan or reserve one untouched final confirmation. A conjunction of predeclared NI/quality tests can be handled by an intersection-union argument; that does not make separately displayed confidence bounds jointly valid. If simultaneous bounds are promised, allocate their error explicitly.
7. **Distinguish sample floors from population quality.** Observed ≥90% aggregate and ≥80% per family are useful acceptance rules, not confidence guarantees. A claim of true accuracy at those levels needs corresponding valid lower bounds and multiplicity handling. Do not translate an observed family pass into proven nonregression for that category.

This supports, at most, a scoped statement about a frozen finite task distribution and declared execution regime. It does not guarantee arbitrary API providers/subscriptions, new task families, visual polish, persistent skill learning, or universal superiority. No future protocol has been adopted by this review.
