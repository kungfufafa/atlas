# Native MiMo statistical scope review

This is a planning review of the current preregistrations, analyzer definitions and declared schedule counts. No live MiMo scored outcomes, attempt records or holdout task contents were opened, and no tests, simulations, inference or control edits were performed. I am **not globally blinded**: inherited context includes earlier DeepSeek development/native-memory evidence and offline instrumentation results. The conclusions below do not use a new MiMo outcome.

The design supports complete, useful descriptive comparisons on its declared fixture corpus. It does not establish that its nominal bootstrap confidence levels have adequate coverage or power for a 5 percentage-point noninferiority margin. This is an adequacy limitation, not evidence that the recorded experiments, fixed scores or source provenance are invalid.

## What the current rule actually establishes

| Confirmatory comparison | Distinct family clusters | Instances per family | Repeats per instance | Paired trajectories | Decision interval |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Native files | 9 | 3 | 2 | 54 | Two-sided 95% family percentile bootstrap |
| Warm memory, each condition | 8 | 3 | 2 | 48 | Two-sided 97.5% family percentile bootstrap |

Memory has two warm conditions: 96 warm pairs total. Another 48 cold/identity pairs are separately reported diagnostics, giving 144 total memory pairs. They do not increase the eight clusters supporting either warm-quality claim. Development has 18 file pairs and 48 memory pairs including diagnostics, and remains exploratory. Counts come from the preregs, not observed executions.

The analyzer averages repetitions within each instance, then instances within a family, and resamples the resulting whole-family effects. This avoids treating repeated seeds as independent families and preserves equal family weighting. It uses 100,000 draws, but those draws add Monte Carlo precision, not new experimental information. The inferential sample is eight or nine clusters, not 48/54 pairs, 96/108 harness trajectories, or the number of provider calls.

The code correctly blocks qualifying claims from development/pilots, diagnostic groups, missing/duplicated arms, provenance failures, uncertain mandatory usage/time, and a degenerate family interval. Degeneracy blocks even identical positive family effects; it is a conservative information gate, not proof of no difference. Its `informationAdequate` flag checks the expected cluster count and nondegeneracy only. That name must not be read as a power calculation or validation of confidence coverage.

## Adequacy concerns and interpretation limits

- **Few curated clusters and an unclear sampling target.** Eight/nine deliberately chosen synthetic families are not a documented random sample of daily work. Some share formats, infrastructure and model behavior. Treating their effects as exchangeable independent draws needs an assumption not established by a nonzero interval. Family bootstrapping can be descriptive sensitivity to the family mix; it is not automatically a calibrated confidence interval for a fixed nine-family corpus, future stochastic executions, or an unspecified universe of tasks. Three new numeric/seed variants do not constitute unseen task families.
- **Within-family uncertainty remains limited.** Only three seeded instances and two correlated repetitions estimate each family effect. Resampling just collapsed family means cannot recover unobserved within-family/run variability. Repetition averaging is appropriate for weighting, but does not prove that eight/nine cluster percentile intervals have nominal tail coverage. No coverage/power calibration is declared. Small cluster count alone does not make every possible result invalid; it prevents treating nominal coverage as established.
- **Floors are sample rules, not accuracy confidence guarantees.** Each confirmatory family has six paired executions, so the 80% Atlas floor means at least 5/6 observed successes (83.3%). The 90% aggregate floor means at least 49/54 file successes or 44/48 successes in a memory condition under the balanced complete schedule. These are coarse observed thresholds. Even assuming six independent trials, a true 70% success rate yields six successes with probability 0.7^6 ≈ 11.8%; passing a tiny family's observed floor cannot certify true family accuracy of at least 80%. Repetition correlation supplies no rescue.
- **The 5pp margin is provisional.** It is a declared tolerance, not a demonstrated product requirement, power target or equivalence margin. A noninferiority result would exclude being worse by 5pp under the interval's assumptions; it would not establish exact equality, absence of regression in every task, or superiority. Equivalence requires a separate two-sided tolerance claim. Point delta zero and failure to reject a difference establish neither.
- **Multiplicity is bounded, not absent.** Two memory intervals at 97.5% implement the stated within-memory correction. Because the decisions use only lower bounds, their nominal tail allocations are 0.0125 each; the file 95% interval contributes 0.025. Thus these three fixed directional decisions have a nominal union-bound allocation of 0.05 *if each lower bound has valid coverage*. It would be incorrect to claim that cross-track directional multiplicity is necessarily uncontrolled here. The same interval's NI and superiority thresholds are nested and need no second independent-test charge. However, the three full two-sided intervals are not automatically jointly 95%, and this accounting does not cover selecting a headline across additional models, future studies/candidates, subgroups, endpoints or secondary outcomes. Bootstrap miscoverage is not repaired by Bonferroni arithmetic.

No formal hypothesis-test implementation discrepancy was identified in these frozen definitions. The risky interpretation would be upgrading `qualifies_within_scope` to a statistically established guarantee solely because the code emits it. A defensible current report gives raw matched counts, all family deltas, observed floors, the nominal interval and its assumptions; any qualification remains provisional and restricted to the exact synthetic family mix, model/header, capabilities and budget. Unknown accounting cannot be relabeled as evidence that either product's reasoning or memory is worse.

## What would require a new prospective study

Before claiming a calibrated 5pp NI guarantee, define the target population and estimand: a fixed weighted list of families under repeated stochastic execution, or performance on newly sampled work families. Then justify the tolerance from practical consequences, allocate the claim family across tracks/models, and choose an analysis with demonstrated coverage for that design. Pre-outcome simulation or analytic calibration should include plausible baseline success rates, paired discordance, within-family/repetition correlation, heterogeneous effects and shared transport variation. Set sample size to a stated precision/power target; this review does not invent a universal minimum family count.

For generalization across work, collect more independently sourced families and substantially different instances from the declared population; extra repeats of the same templates are insufficient. For inference restricted to the fixed families, increase independent instances and use a preregistered method that represents their nested stochastic uncertainty. More repeated runs can characterize model variation, but must remain nested and must not inflate independent sample count. Any changed corpus, method, margin, multiplicity plan or sample-size rule belongs in a new version before its outcomes are inspected; do not loosen current thresholds, extend until significant, drop failures or reuse inspected confirmation as untouched.

Universal Atlas-versus-Hermes superiority, API/subscription/provider parity, autonomous skill learning, visual polish and causal product improvement remain outside these studies regardless of sample size or a passing native interval. A single MiMo model/header stratum cannot establish those claims.
