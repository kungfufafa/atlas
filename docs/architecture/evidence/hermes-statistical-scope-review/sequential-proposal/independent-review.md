# Independent review of the prospective sequential option

**Verdict: the stated supermartingale argument is valid under its conditional-mean null. It is not yet an adopted or fully specified experiment, and it cannot be substituted for the existing fixed-quota studies.** Source and hypothetical numeric-output inspection only; no live outcomes, ledger, holdout, test, simulation, or provider execution was used.

## Mathematics

For `X ∈ {-1,0,1}`, `δ=.05`, and a fixed `λ ∈ [0,1]`, the increment is at least `1−.95λ ≥ .05`. Under `E[X_t | F_(t−1)] ≤ −.05`, its conditional expectation is at most one. Multiplying prior nonnegative wealth preserves the supermartingale inequality. A prespecified weighted average of the eight processes starts at one and preserves that property; its crossing threshold `1/α` has the asserted anytime type-I bound. No asymptotics, paired-binomial approximation or independence between the two arms is needed for this argument. Dependence between successive pairs is permissible only insofar as the conditional-mean null still holds.

This is consistent with the primary bounded-mean betting framework's conditional-mean, predictable-bet, mixture and Ville arguments. The paper's without-replacement extension uses additional structure; it does not validate replacing that structure with this simple factor under a shuffled quota design. [Waudby-Smith and Ramdas, Sections 2 and 4–5](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/86/1/1/7043257).

Rejecting this null establishes a directional NI conclusion relative to −5 percentage points, within the sampling model. It does not establish equality, superiority over zero, or two-sided equivalence. Revise “both directional tests” to “one one-sided NI test for each prespecified endpoint” if that is what is intended; a reverse-direction test is a different hypothesis.

The calculator uses the declared equal-weight average via log-sum-exp and `log1p`, checks the threshold after each observation, and includes losses rather than only successful pairs. Its saved 117/132 all-tie crossings are illustrative paths, not power or a sample-size recommendation. Deterministic periodic paths are not null-distribution simulations: their phase can imply conditional means above the null on particular steps even if a full-period mean is −.05. “No crossing in this periodic example” is therefore not a type-I validation test. I inspected the code/output relationship but did not rerun the calculator or independently reproduce every saved crossing count.

## Requirements before a confirmatory use

1. **State the filtration and target precisely.** Define `F_(t−1)` to include all information used to select/admit the next task, vary stakes, or stop. A sufficient operational design draws a fresh family and instance with replacement after previous enrolled evidence is finalized, using locked weights and candidate/provider settings. Under a stable execution regime this supplies a fixed weighted catalog mean. Do not silently condition on a chosen family and still assume the aggregate-mean null applies within that family. Presampled tasks may be possible under a separately specified nonadaptive filtration, but outcome-dependent scheduling/skipping or inspecting future labels to make inclusion decisions must not be hand-waved away. Fresh runtime directories alone do not establish these assumptions.
2. **Keep the fixed-quota incompatibility explicit.** Some family conditional means can exceed −.05 while the equal-family aggregate is below it. Shuffling a fixed quota does not make this stepwise null true. If exact family balance is essential, a complete independently sampled equal-weight catalog block can be one bounded `X` observation, and wealth updates only after that entire block settles. That is a different unit, with much larger cost per update; its own inter-block conditional-mean assumption and exposure analysis are required.
3. **Do not drop unresolved pairs under “only finalized pairs update.”** Predeclare enrollment, terminal deadlines and a rule for every enrolled pair. Fast-result ordering, lost records, or excluding inconvenient partial runs can bias the sequence. Never update with a known favorable arm before the matched arm is resolved. If the scientific target is underlying factual correctness and evidence is missing, a robust option is the least-favorable difference `X_lower = A_lower − H_upper`, using 0 for unobserved Atlas correctness and 1 for unobserved Hermes correctness. It is pointwise no greater than the true difference, so its test is conservative for that target without a missing-at-random assumption. Alternatively define timely, observable completion as the endpoint from the outset; then failure to observe can be a zero, but the estimand is operational completion, not latent factual accuracy. Merely logging excluded attempts is not an inferential repair.
4. **Separate endpoint meanings.** The earlier counterexample still applies: 90% both correct/certified, plus 10% Atlas wrong and Hermes correct-but-uncertified, yields certified rates 90/90 while factual rates are 90/100. Sequential validity does not repair that construct mismatch. A useful-work NI claim therefore needs the prespecified factual/delivered-correctness guard as well as certified completion; unknown cost remains unknown and cannot be filled in from successful-call averages.
5. **Specify multiplicity and candidate selection.** Allocate error across newly tried candidates, models, endpoint profiles, chosen conditions and separately advertised conclusions. Do not restart a process at wealth one without an additional budget. For a single fixed claim that is strictly a conjunction of all endpoint requirements, intersection-union testing can use level α for each component: if the overall claim is false, at least one component null is true and joint rejection is contained in its rejection event. This does not license separate simultaneous component claims or selecting whichever condition passes; those need their own familywise allocation. A conservative explicit allocation is easier to audit, though potentially less efficient. No numeric split is selected here.
6. **Keep quality and relevance gates honest.** Sample rates ≥90% aggregate/≥80% per family are observed quality screens; optional stopping does not make them lower confidence guarantees on population quality. If the headline promises those population floors, add prespecified one-sided confidence-sequence/tests for them and account for the declared claim structure. A random-mixture sample can leave some families poorly represented; minimum family coverage is a transparent operational prerequisite, not proof of within-family power. Lock an operator-relevant task frame independently of candidate outcomes, rather than repeatedly drawing from an easy handpicked catalog.
7. **Stop for evidence or a prespecified practical bound.** A maximum budget and nonbinding futility stop do not invalidate the crossing guarantee if all enrolled evidence/rules are preserved. They can leave the result inconclusive. Do not turn the hypothetical tie counts into promised exposure, and do not change the task frame or mixture after a disappointing run. Later delivery/cost gates must also remain prospective; a claim can be withheld after statistical crossing if integrity evidence fails.

## Recommended next option

If root later adopts a sequential study, use one fixed candidate and one exact model/header profile; an operator-reviewed frozen frame; independently drawn weighted family/instance pairs with fresh within-pair state; fixed adjacent arm randomization; all-enrolled terminal bookkeeping; and separate one-sided processes for factual delivery and certified completion. Freeze the mixture, error allocation, practical maximum, qualification language and required family coverage before any new scored calls. If balanced family quotas are a hard requirement, use full-catalog blocks or retain the earlier fixed-n design instead of claiming this per-pair proof applies.

No source protocol is modified by this review. The recommendations are conditional design requirements, not a guarantee for arbitrary providers/subscriptions, a validated power calculation, or a claim about the current Atlas/Hermes results.
